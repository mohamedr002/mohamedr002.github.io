Our Vision

Building the full stack of industrial AI

We build the full stack of industrial AI — from sensor signals to autonomous decisions. Our work bridges foundation models, physics-informed deep learning, generative AI, and agentic systems, applied across high-stakes industrial domains: aerospace and MRO (jet engines, eVTOLs, drones), autonomous mobility (electric and self-driving vehicles), maritime and energy assets, and modern manufacturing.

The lab positions itself alongside applied industrial-AI groups (e.g., the ZHAW Rieter Industrial AI Group) with a distinct angle: where they emphasize neuro-symbolic AI and learning-based control, we focus on foundation-model and physics-informed deep learning approaches for physical industrial assets.

Research Pillars

Five thrusts that anchor the lab's research agenda

Physics-Informed Machine Learning

Embedding physical laws — thermodynamics, degradation dynamics, and material fatigue — into deep learning architectures for trustworthy, generalizable predictions on industrial assets.

Industrial AI Foundation Models

Pretraining transformer models on diverse multi-source PHM data (vibration, thermal, pressure, acoustic) to enable few-shot transfer across machines, conditions, and failure modes.

Accelerated Scientific Discovery

Surrogate models, neural operators, and active learning loops that compress design and qualification cycles for new materials, components, and engineering systems.

Intelligent Health Monitoring

Prognostics and health management across aerospace (jet engines, eVTOLs, drones), MRO operations, autonomous and electric vehicles, ships, and manufacturing assets. Anomaly detection, fault diagnosis, and remaining useful life estimation at fleet scale.

Generative AI for Industrial Applications

LLMs, RAG, and agentic systems for industrial operations and maintenance. Multi-agent architectures that turn raw sensor signals into actionable maintenance decisions and work orders.

The Full Stack

Pillars connect end-to-end: sensor → foundation model → physics-aware reasoning → edge deployment → autonomous agent action. Few groups span this entire chain.

Current Projects

Active and in-flight work under the lab umbrella

PHM-FM — Foundation Model for Industrial Prognostics

Pretraining transformer foundation models on multi-source PHM datasets (C-MAPSS, NASA bearings, XJTU-SY, FEMTO, plus TII jet-engine data) for few-shot transfer to unseen equipment. In progress, NeurIPS / ICML 2026 cycle.

PhysRUL — Physics-Constrained Transformers for RUL

Injecting thermodynamic, monotonic-degradation, and material-fatigue priors into transformer architectures for trustworthy and OOD-robust remaining-useful-life estimation. Targeting AAAI 2027.

IndustrialAgent — Agentic AI for Autonomous Diagnosis

Multi-agent architecture: edge SLMs monitor sensors, reasoning LLMs diagnose via RAG over digital twins, planning agents generate work orders. Designing for KDD / ACM CAIS 2027.

LLM/RAG for Operations & Maintenance

Domain-specific LLMs and retrieval pipelines for querying maintenance records, fault logs, and operational manuals. Active TII engagement.

Digital Twin ML

Simulation-in-the-loop modeling for industrial assets — bridging physics-based simulators and learned components for closed-loop training of agents and surrogates.

Federated Learning for Distributed Assets

Privacy-preserving federated transfer learning for time series across distributed industrial sites — manufacturing, healthcare, IoT — without sharing raw data. Funded by A*STAR CDF.
